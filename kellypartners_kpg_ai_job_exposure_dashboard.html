Key Finding — The Institutional V-Score Cliff

Kelly Partners is the portfolio's cleanest illustration of the Institutional constraint. Tax compliance tasks score F=4, A=5, S=5 — AI can draft every return, parse every receipt, and compute every deduction. But V=1 across all sign-off tasks, because the Chartered Accountant who signs carries personal legal liability under the Tax Agent Services Act and Corporations Act. This single FAVES dimension — consequence volatility — drags 63.5% of all executive tasks into the Augment quadrant. The technology is ready; the regulation isn't. KPG's Automate Now (4.1%) is virtually identical to GenusPlus (4.0%, an infrastructure company), despite operating in a completely different sector. The binding constraint has shifted from physics to law.

The Sign-Off Paradox

Paul Kuchta and Ada Poon — both practising Chartered Accountants and Executive Directors — show 0% Automate Now and 75%/60% Augment respectively. AI can prepare a client's entire tax return, BAS, and SMSF compliance pack. But the moment someone clicks "lodge" with the ATO, a licensed professional must have verified the output. This is not a technology problem — it's a regulatory architecture problem. Until the ATO and ASIC create frameworks for AI-verified compliance (as APRA has begun exploring for banking), the Human Fortress in professional services is defined by statute, not by capability.

The Asymmetric FAVES Signature

Kelly Partners shows the most lopsided FAVES radar in the portfolio. F (Fidelity), A (Autonomy), and S (Decomposability) score 3-5 across most tasks — the tax compliance workflow is highly structured, fully digital (Xero/MYOB), and breaks into verifiable steps. But V (Volatility of consequence) collapses to 1-2 because an incorrect tax return triggers ATO penalties, client financial harm, and potential professional deregistration. And E (Entanglement) is low because lodging requires authenticated access to ATO portals, client accounting systems, and practice management platforms. This lopsided pentagon — strong on three axes, collapsed on two — is the Institutional constraint's fingerprint.

Adoption Gap — Theoretical vs Practical

Joyce Au's 90-Point Gap — The Governance Paradox

The Group Company Secretary shows the largest adoption gap in the entire 3-company portfolio: 90% of tasks are theoretically feasible (FAVES ≥ 3.0), but 0% are in Automate Now. The gap will close only when regulatory frameworks evolve to accept AI-prepared governance documentation with human attestation, similar to how e-signatures displaced wet-ink requirements.

Strategic Response & Value Chain Escalation

Portfolio Allocation for KPG C-Suite

With 4.1% Automate Now and dominant Augment zone, the recommended allocation is: 10% Operator / 50% Fortress / 40% Escalate — identical to GenusPlus's recommendation despite radically different sectors. The shared pattern: when the constraint is structural (physical or institutional), fortress and escalation dominate.

Become the Operator

10%

Company Secretary can deploy AI for Board pack preparation and regulatory monitoring. At practice level (below C-suite scope), AI-assisted tax return preparation is already technically feasible and should be deployed to increase billable utilisation — the accountant reviews and signs, AI prepares.

🏰

Lean Into the Fortress

50%

This is KPG's primary strategy. The fortress is: (1) Brett Kelly's M&A acquisition judgment — the ability to identify, negotiate, and integrate accounting practices that no algorithm can replicate; (2) Paul Kuchta and Ada Poon's client relationships — 20+ years of trust with SME owners who rely on their accountant for life decisions, not just compliance; (3) The CPA liability wrapper — as long as a licensed professional must sign, the fortress holds; (4) The Partner-Owner cultural model — motivation and accountability structures that AI cannot replicate. Double down on relationship depth, succession planning for partner network, and the human judgment layer that converts AI-prepared work into signed-off professional advice.

🚀

Escalate the Value Chain

40%

AI commoditises tax preparation — the commodity layer. KPG must escalate: (1) From compliance to advisory — use AI-freed capacity to deliver higher-value business structuring, succession planning, and outsourced CFO services at premium rates; (2) From annual returns to continuous intelligence — AI-generated real-time financial dashboards for SME clients become a subscription service, not an annual event; (3) From individual advice to network value — 23,000 SME clients create a data network effect that competitors cannot replicate; deploy AI-powered benchmarking across the client base. The pattern: AI does the $3,000 tax return, the accountant redirects 6 hours to a $15,000 business advisory engagement.

Methodology & Attribution

IMPACT × FAVES Framework

Developed by Walter Adamson (OutcomesNow). This dual-axis framework separates the business case for AI automation (IMPACT) from the technical feasibility of generative AI execution (FAVES), producing a 2×2 decision matrix that is substantially more actionable than single-composite scoring approaches.

Inspired by the Trust Insights TRIPS framework (Christopher S. Penn & Katie Robbert), which pioneered task-level AI prioritisation scoring. IMPACT × FAVES extends this foundation by identifying that most TRIPS dimensions measure economic opportunity rather than technical feasibility, and introducing a dedicated feasibility axis (FAVES) with factors absent from prior frameworks: output verifiability, systems-of-record entanglement, consequence asymmetry, and step decomposability.

Institutional Constraint Analysis

KPG was selected for this portfolio specifically to illustrate the Institutional constraint archetype. Key regulatory barriers affecting FAVES V-scores: (1) Tax Agent Services Act 2009 — requires registered tax agents to exercise professional judgment on all lodged returns; (2) CPA Australia Code of Ethics — professional liability for signed-off work; (3) Corporations Act — director duties for audit opinions and financial statements; (4) ATO practice management requirements — authentication and lodgement controls. These create a V-score floor of 1-2 across all compliance tasks regardless of AI capability advancement. The V-score will only shift when regulatory frameworks explicitly accommodate AI-in-the-loop compliance workflows with modified liability allocation.

Limitations

KPG has a lean corporate team — most of the 650+ employees are practice-level accountants, bookkeepers, and administrators. This analysis covers the 6 identified leadership roles only. Practice-level staff (who deliver the actual accounting services) would show significantly higher Automate Now percentages for preparation tasks, though sign-off tasks would remain in Augment. KPG's US expansion roles are not separately analysed. Brett Kelly's 49% shareholding and combined CEO/Chair role create concentration risk that is a governance consideration but not directly captured in IMPACT × FAVES scoring.
